Key Findings
Islam grows fastest. Muslims are the only major religion projected to grow faster than the world's population overall. By 2050, Christians (2.92B, 31.4%) and Muslims (2.76B, 29.7%) will be nearly equal for the first time in history. Around 2070, Muslims are projected to outnumber Christians globally.
Unaffiliated decline in share. Although the unaffiliated will grow by ~100M in absolute terms, their global share shrinks from 16.4% to 13.2%. In the United States, however, the unaffiliated are projected to grow from 16% to 26% of the population by 2050.
Christianity's net switching loss. Christians are expected to suffer the largest net switching loss—~66 million more people leave Christianity than join it, mostly becoming unaffiliated. The unaffiliated gain a net 61 million through switching.
Sub-Saharan Africa drives growth. Sub-Saharan Africa will go from 12% to 20% of world population by 2050. Four in ten Christians will live there by 2050. Muslim and Christian populations both grow rapidly in the region due to very high fertility (4.8 children/woman on average).
Buddhism is the only shrinking group. Buddhists are projected to decline by ~0.3% in absolute number due to low fertility (1.6 children/woman) and aging populations concentrated in China, Japan and Thailand.
India surpasses Indonesia. India will retain a Hindu majority but will have the world's largest Muslim population (~311M) by 2050, surpassing Indonesia. Nigeria is projected to have the world's 3rd-largest Christian population after the US and Brazil.
Europe becomes less Christian. Christians will drop below 50% of the population in 8 countries by 2050, including France, the Netherlands, and the UK. The Muslim share of Europe's population will rise from 5.9% (2010) to 10.2% (2050).
Muslim youth bulge is decisive. 34% of Muslims worldwide were under 15 in 2010—the highest of any group. Combined with a global fertility rate of 3.1 (vs world average 2.5), this demographic momentum is the primary driver of Islamic population growth.
Source: Pew Research Center · The Future of World Religions: Population Growth Projections, 2010–2050 · April 2, 2015 · www.pewresearch.org
